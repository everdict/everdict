# Finding dispositions

What happened to what the reviewer and the scanner reported. Written by
`pnpm findings --record`, and COMMITTED: a judgement nobody else can read is one the next person makes again.

A finding is `real` (it named a defect), `false-positive` (it did not), or `carried` (real, and deliberately
not fixed here — an intent holds it). Precision counts real and carried against the total; carrying a finding
is not the same as disagreeing with it.

## ⚠️ Read the 97% with what produced it

A precision this high is not a claim that the reviewer is nearly always right. It is a fact about WHICH
findings are in the corpus and WHEN they were graded, and both bias it upward:

- **The corpus is findings somebody already acted on.** Almost every `real` below was verified by opening the
  file and finding the repair already there, often with the incident recorded in the code's own comment. A
  reviewer's findings that nobody agreed with tend not to survive as a report at all.
- **They were graded in one pass, months after the fact.** "Was this defect repaired?" is a much easier
  question than "is this a defect?", and it is the question a late grading actually answers. Grading at the
  moment of the review — while the disagreement is live — is the harder and more informative version.

What the number IS good for is its direction over time and its counter-metric: `false-positive` counts the
reviewer's cost, and two of sixty-three is the measurement that was missing entirely before this ledger. Read
it beside how many findings each run raises, never alone.
