# R32 — OfficeQA: tracing exact answers through a large document corpus

OfficeQA evaluates grounded reasoning over dense financial documents: find the right source, extract the right values, apply the requested calculation, and return a precisely formatted answer. The bibliography cites Databricks' 9 December 2025 launch article, a technical resource rather than the later research report. This note explains all major launch experiments and separately identifies changes in the official repository inspected on 13 September 2026. It does not mix the original 246-question results with newer OfficeQA Pro or Pro V2 scores.[^1]

## Why grounded reasoning needs a separate benchmark

A capable mathematical model can still fail when the numbers it needs are buried in a scanned table. Conversely, retrieving a page with matching keywords is insufficient if its columns use fiscal years while the question asks about calendar years. The task is a chain of dependent operations: source discovery, evidence interpretation, calculation, and answer serialization.

This differs from HLE's expert academic questions, GDPval's broad professional deliverables, and abstract pattern-reasoning tests. OfficeQA emphasizes diligence and precision on a large corpus, with automatically checkable answers. The Treasury documents are public but serve as a proxy for difficult enterprise collections; their public availability does not make them an actual sample of private customer records.

For Everdict, the benchmark can reveal whether an evolved procedure improves evidence handling. It can also reveal a misleading apparent improvement when the parser, corpus, retrieval access, or grader changes alongside the candidate.

## Corpus, task construction, and version identity

The launch benchmark contains 246 questions over roughly 89,000 pages of US Treasury Bulletins. Earlier issues are scans, with digitally produced PDFs appearing later. Questions average about two source documents. A sampled human study reports roughly fifty minutes per question, largely spent finding information; the article does not provide enough sampling and variance detail to treat that as a universal human baseline.[^1]

The authors work with USAFacts to identify useful sources and questions, seek one checkable answer per question, and filter questions that models can answer without the corpus. They report only about 2% accuracy without access. This filtering makes the collection intentionally retrieval-dependent; it does not prove absence of all memorization or future contamination.

Difficulty labels depend on baseline-agent outcomes. The launch caption names a 113-question hard subset, while today's repository defines Pro as 133 hard questions and Full as those 133 plus 113 easy questions. The launch's verbal easy/hard rule and its reported strict baseline scores also do not fully specify one reproducible partition. These counts and labels must be versioned rather than treated as interchangeable.

At inspected repository revision `7b9a3c154ef9fb40215bb67934afc43e6799de16`, the suite contains the following:[^2]

| Current release | Questions | Corpus and purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| OfficeQA Full | 246 | Treasury Bulletins; development across difficulty levels |
| OfficeQA Pro | 133 | Hard subset of Full; same 697-issue Treasury corpus |
| OfficeQA Pro V2 | 90 | Different 1,435-document receipts/expenditures corpus |

The V2 corpus spans 1793–2024, compared with 1939–2025 for the listed Treasury corpus. V2 questions use a median 5.5 source documents, with a range of one to twenty-four. This is a useful generalization direction, but new corpus, older documents, and retrieval depth change together. A lower V2 score cannot be attributed to one factor alone.

## The four launch evaluation conditions

The article tests two complete agent systems and two oracle-evidence conditions for each model.[^1]

The GPT-5.1 agent uses high reasoning effort through the Responses API, with file and web search. PDFs enter a vector store that parses and indexes them. The Claude Opus 4.5 agent uses its Agent SDK, high thinking, local files, search tools, and code execution; it can install PDF-reading packages. Their native capabilities differ, so this is a system comparison rather than a model-only controlled experiment.

For each agent, a second input condition supplies documents parsed with Databricks' `ai_parse_document`. Existing PDF OCR layers are removed in all experiments because of their poor accuracy. That preprocessing choice is part of the benchmark configuration: results cannot simply be transferred to a corpus retaining the original OCR.

The non-agentic oracle conditions provide exactly the PDF pages needed for the answer, either as PDF pages or pre-parsed text. They remove document search but also lack the agents' full tool workflow. Calling them a strict upper bound is too strong: an agent can use computation, external lookup, or additional evidence that the non-agentic oracle model lacks. They are diagnostic controls for evidence access and representation.

## Results from the launch figures

At zero allowed relative error, the figures give these full-benchmark correctness values. They are historical source results, not newly reproduced measurements.[^3]

| Model/system | PDF input | Databricks-parsed input | Gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GPT-5.1, oracle pages | 38.1% | 70.5% | +32.4 points |
| Claude Opus 4.5, oracle pages | 55.1% | 59.1% | +4.0 points |
| GPT-5.1 agent, full corpus | 43.5% | 52.6% | +9.1 points |
| Claude Opus 4.5 agent, full corpus | 37.4% | 67.6% | +30.2 points |

The raw-PDF hard-subset figure gives Claude 21.1% and GPT-5.1 24.8%. Parsing changes the relative ordering of the full-corpus agents, while the oracle-page improvement differs substantially by model. An apparent model advantage can therefore depend strongly on document representation and the harness's ability to inspect it.

The figure is more precise than several prose summaries: oracle GPT-5.1 reaches 70.5%, despite broad introductory wording about systems staying below 70%. Some prose relative-improvement percentages also do not exactly match the displayed endpoints. The table above preserves the plotted values and absolute-point differences.

The stronger parsed Claude agent takes about five minutes per question, compared with about three for the lower-scoring OpenAI agent in the article. This is latency context, not a complete economic comparison: parsing/indexing preparation, concurrency, cached documents, failures, and monetary costs need separate accounting. The launch report does not provide uncertainty intervals or a detailed repeated-run protocol for every curve.

## Tolerance curves and the difference between near and correct

The launch evaluation varies allowable absolute relative error. For a nonzero reference y and prediction p, the conceptual numeric condition is `|p − y| / |y| ≤ tolerance`. At zero tolerance, parsed-agent correctness is 67.6% for Claude and 52.6% for GPT-5.1; at 5%, it is 83.0% and 69.2%. The curves also show 0.1% and 1% settings.[^3]

A tolerance curve diagnoses numerical sensitivity, but a nearly correct number can still use the wrong source. Mixing fiscal and calendar years, omitting a revision, or associating a cell with the wrong header can produce small or large errors. A relaxed tolerance does not certify the reasoning path.

Two source examples illustrate this. Using an earlier rather than revised bulletin produces a 21-million-dollar discrepancy, only about 0.01% of the reference. Mixing fiscal and calendar values in a fitted time series produces a much larger error, reported as 286,831 million dollars or 31.6%. The practical meaning of an error depends on the question's contract, not only a benchmark-wide percentage threshold.

Everdict should therefore retain strict correctness, tolerance sensitivity, and source/procedure validity separately. A candidate must not improve by silently changing the tolerance or returning a unit convention that the evaluator happens to accept.

## What today's scoring code actually checks

The current repository's `reward.py` is a deterministic heuristic scorer, and it is not established here as the exact launch evaluator. Its public entry point extracts the last `FINAL_ANSWER` block if present, then checks a short direct-answer shape before numeric/text matching. It rejects multiple nonempty lines, answers above 250 characters, and inappropriate numeric arity. Bracketed numeric lists receive an ordered matching path.[^4]

The code normalizes several punctuation/currency formats and handles some year-like numbers specially. Numeric units are compared as labels around base numbers; it does not generally convert one million into one million units for equivalence. Thus a zero-tolerance score is a specified parser behavior, not unrestricted mathematical or semantic equivalence.

Small local probes against the pinned scorer confirm both useful checks and limits:

| Reference | Prediction | Score at zero tolerance |
|---|---|---:|
| `[1, 2]` | `[2, 1]` | 0 |
| `1 million` | `1000000` | 0 |
| `1 million` | `1` | 1 |
| `123.45` | `The answer is not 123.45.` | 1 |

These are this investigation's synthetic scorer checks, not model benchmark results. The last case shows that matching the number can accept a negated statement. It does not establish how frequently actual model outputs exploit the behavior. A deterministic grader avoids LLM judgment variability but still needs contract tests and error audits.

## Failure analysis and the limits of the source

The launch article describes extraction errors in nested or merged tables, ambiguous revisions, premature stopping after a plausible value, and failures on charts. Approximately 3% of questions involve visual figures and about 13% need external lookup in the source's analysis. This helps explain why relevant pages alone do not guarantee success.[^1]

These categories are observations from development, not a randomized decomposition of all errors. The parser intervention changes a substantial part of the input pipeline, and the report comes from the parser's provider. A comparison against alternative parsers, shared indexing, and identical tool budgets would strengthen attribution; the reported gains remain evidence for the tested systems.

A proposed Gemini baseline is excluded because roughly 30% of documents fail ingestion and the file-search tool cannot be combined with the needed web-search tool in that tested setup. This is an integration limitation at the time, not a measured zero score or a permanent claim about Gemini's reasoning ability.

The current suite stores large data on Hugging Face behind access gating intended to prevent browsing agents from reaching answer keys. Its metadata includes source filenames and pages. Those fields are useful for evaluation and oracle experiments but become privileged hints if exposed to an end-to-end retrieval agent. Even the repository's convenience suggestion to download only referenced V2 documents changes the candidate corpus from the full retrieval problem.

## An Everdict evolution experiment for grounded numerical work

Evolve a procedure that creates an evidence table before calculating: document edition, page, row/column meaning, units, fiscal/calendar basis, revision status, and extracted value. Require calculations to reference those entries and final-answer formatting to follow the declared contract. Compare no procedure, a fixed procedure, and an evolved procedure at matched search and execution budgets. This is a proposed experiment, not an implemented Everdict feature.

Cross the procedure conditions with fixed parser representations to distinguish gains from better extraction from gains from better reasoning. Maintain a full-corpus condition and an explicitly labeled oracle-page condition. Do not let source-file metadata or answer fields leak into the full-corpus input. Freeze corpus, parser, index, evaluator, and candidate digests before confirmation.

Use family-aware splits covering document editions, overlapping source tables, and near-identical calculation templates. Retain raw page images and parsed coordinates so an incorrect number can be traced to extraction, selection, or calculation. Curated corrections may update a derived evidence view, but the raw source and previous interpretation remain in the ledger.

Confirm gains on strict answers and source fidelity, then report relaxed tolerances as diagnostics. A second corpus can test whether a learned procedure transfers, provided the candidate stops evolving before that confirmation. OfficeQA's strongest lesson is that reliable evolution must improve the entire evidence-to-answer chain while keeping every change to that chain identifiable.
