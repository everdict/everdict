# R43 — Self-Harness: model-specific edits, regression gates, and evidence quality

Self-Harness v3 asks whether a fixed model can improve the non-parametric system controlling its own execution. The same model solves tasks and proposes bounded harness edits; an external evaluation process mines failures and decides promotion. All nine model/benchmark combinations improve in the reported search. Its strongest relevance to Everdict is the explicit relationship between evidence, declared edits, evaluated behavior, and version lineage. Its largest methodological limitation is that the repeatedly queried “held-out” split also selects candidates.[^1]

## Background and the object that evolves

A harness includes prompts, tools, memory, state handling, verification guidance, recovery policies, and runtime control. Changing it can repair missing artifacts or repeated tool failures without changing model weights. ReAct supplies an execution pattern; Reflexion changes memory for later attempts; ADAS and MetaHarness search agent designs or code. Self-Harness emphasizes a narrower self-proposal setting: the evaluated model, under its current harness, proposes changes to declared configuration surfaces.[^2]

Self-improvement here is not unsupervised authority over the entire system. The model and evaluator remain fixed, and another process runs evaluations and applies the acceptance rule. External evidence construction is a substantial part of the method. A better final harness does not demonstrate autonomous improvement without that evaluator infrastructure.

The baseline is deliberately minimal, built on DeepAgent with a short task-facing prompt and filesystem/shell tools. It is not a tuned production agent. The editable harness definition contains hooks for bootstrap, execution, verification, recovery, subagents, and runtime policies. The measured gains are therefore gains over this specific sparse starting point.

## Weakness mining: separate the observed failure from its explanation

For each failed held-in execution, the method records the task, output, trace, and verifier result. It assigns a failure signature with three parts: terminal verifier cause, the causal status of an agent behavior, and an abstract mechanism. Records with identical signatures form a cluster, ordered by support and estimated actionability.[^3]

For example, a missing file at final evaluation might result from never starting implementation, deleting the artifact during recovery, or writing it to the wrong location. The same final symptom can require different fixes. The evidence bundle includes representative traces and passing behaviors to preserve; it does not prescribe an edit.

Exact-signature grouping is deterministic once signatures exist. The signature assignment and causal interpretation are not thereby proven deterministic or correct. The paper supplies no detailed attribution-accuracy study or human agreement estimate. Fixed models and evaluators also do not remove stochastic generation or environmental variation, so a before/after trace is supporting evidence rather than a controlled causal proof by itself.

## Proposal diversity, bounded edits, and audit records

The proposer receives allowed edit surfaces, clustered failure evidence, behavior to preserve, and previous-attempt summaries. Several branches propose materially different mechanisms or intervention surfaces from the same evidence. Each bundle contains a patch and an audit record explaining the target failure, expected effect, and regression risk.[^3]

The intended discipline is diversity across branches and minimal change within a branch. Minimality is described semantically, however; there is no universal numerical diff-size or behavioral-distance bound. A short middleware condition can affect every tool failure, and adding a subagent can change runtime computation substantially.

Failures that are unstable, task-specific, or outside the editable surface should not force a patch. This is a useful separation between diagnosing a weakness and asserting that the current search space can fix it. Rejected candidates are logged, preserving negative evidence for later proposals.

## The acceptance rule and the meaning of held-out

Each candidate is evaluated on fixed held-in and held-out sets. Let Δin and Δout be changes in aggregate passed attempts. Accept if both are nonnegative and at least one is positive. Equal scores on both splits are rejected; a net overall gain cannot compensate for a decrease in either split.[^4]

Candidates normally receive two repeated attempts per task, and the rule applies to aggregate counts. The reported pass rate is mean success per attempt, not success at least once out of two. Two attempts reduce some noise but do not establish statistical significance or eliminate winner's selection bias.

The held-out examples are not shown to the proposer, but their outcomes repeatedly determine acceptance. This makes the split a **selection/regression set**, not an untouched final confirmation set. Learning through accept/reject decisions is still adaptive use of its information. The fact that promoted versions do not decrease aggregate scores is partly guaranteed by the selection rule and cannot independently validate generalization.

Aggregate non-regression also permits some previously solved tasks to fail if other tasks compensate. The paper does not guarantee per-task preservation. Compatible accepted edits can be merged, but the pseudocode does not explicitly re-evaluate the merged artifact before adoption. Plotted merged candidates have scores; reproducing the exact merge-testing and rollback procedure remains necessary because individually beneficial patches can interact badly.

## Benchmarks, models, and budgets

The three models are MiniMax M2.5, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, and GLM-5. Each acts as its own proposer. MiniMax and GLM use hosted services; Qwen runs on four H200 GPUs with an SGLang-derived image. Within comparisons, task sets, backends, non-editable settings, and official evaluators are fixed.[^5]

Terminal-Bench uses sixty-four of the original eighty-nine tasks, excluding unreliable external resources and unsupported multimodal tasks. A 2 MB/s outbound cap and mirrors for some slow resources alter the environment relative to an unrestricted official run. These exclusions are reasonable for control but narrow the benchmark claim.

SWE-bench Verified uses 100 repository-proportionally sampled tasks, split 67 held-in and 33 held-out, with fresh Docker workspaces and official per-instance evaluation. Case and tool timeouts are 3,600 and 120 seconds. This is not the full 500-case benchmark or a repository-disjoint generalization test.

AppWorld uses 180 tasks: ninety official training tasks as held-in and ninety proportionally sampled normal/challenge test tasks as held-out. State resets for every task and the turn cap is fifty. The evaluation measures simulated app state and completion API behavior, not real external transactions.

Evolution plots show differing search lengths, roughly nine to twenty-five candidate evaluations across the displayed runs. The text does not provide a complete uniform proposal-width/round schedule or total token, wall-clock, and monetary cost table. A fixed task budget is distinct from equal total search expense or equal use of subagents inside that budget.

| Terminal / GLM | 46.1 → 57.0 | 42.9 → 57.1 |
| SWE / MiniMax | 46.0 → 52.5 | 34.8 → 40.9 |
| SWE / Qwen | 19.5 → 41.5 | 18.2 → 39.4 |
| SWE / GLM | 52.0 → 55.5 | 48.5 → 50.0 |
| AppWorld / MiniMax | 48.6 → 58.9 | 45.6 → 55.0 |
| AppWorld / Qwen | 22.5 → 52.2 | 20.0 → 44.4 |

Every final model/benchmark cell improves on both selection splits. The largest absolute overall gain is GLM AppWorld's 40.6 percentage points; the largest relative gain is Qwen AppWorld's approximately 132%. These are different comparisons and should not be interchanged.[^6]

The smaller SWE GLM improvement is 3.5 points overall and 1.5 on held-out. With thirty-three held-out tasks and two attempts, 1.5 points is approximately one additional passed attempt. The paper provides no independent repeated-search confidence interval establishing that such a small selected difference will persist on fresh tasks.

There is no matched main matrix against a human-tuned harness, external optimizer, no-clustering proposer, or no-gate alternative. The results demonstrate improvement from the given baseline under the full procedure, not superiority of self-proposal over those alternatives.

## Terminal edits: artifacts, retries, and persistent environment state

MiniMax's final changes encourage early artifact creation, use correct structured tool-content tags, and inject a redirect after fifty tool messages. Qwen adds dependency prechecks, limits repeated exploration, discourages identical failed-command retries, and introduces error-triggered artifact guidance. GLM emphasizes persistent tool/PATH configuration and moving from exploration to implementation.[^7]

The trace examples show a token-counting task finally writing and reading back its answer file, an ELF extractor surviving repair rather than being deleted at the end, and a build task verifying an alternative download and fixing its render check before concluding. These illustrate behavioral differences, but are selected examples, not a random audit of all gains.

One particularly revealing inconsistency appears in Qwen's middleware. Its predicate is a generic tool-error check, but its message asserts that a file is missing. The shown successful trace triggers it on **“file already exists”**, then removes and recreates the file. The final artifact passes, yet the intermediate diagnosis is false. Everdict should retain the useful recovery hypothesis without promoting this inaccurate error interpretation into a trusted fact.

Similarly, “create every missing file immediately” and “always change something after three exploratory steps” can cause unnecessary or incorrect edits outside the benchmark. Persistent shell configuration depends on actual invocation semantics. Such rules need applicability conditions and negative tests, not direct universal adoption.

## SWE edits: verify executable behavior before submission

MiniMax adds empty-diff inspection and targeted local verification. Qwen combines a patch-verifier subagent with explicit pre-submission testing; GLM adds dependency-aware recovery before rerunning the relevant test. These mechanisms address empty or unverified submissions rather than training new code knowledge.[^8]

The Qwen Django case is particularly concrete: the failing version places an SQLite cast fix at the wrong expression layer and never successfully runs the relevant suite. The revised trace changes the inner compatible expression, runs a forty-nine-test suite with three skips, and passes one FAIL-TO-PASS plus forty-six PASS-TO-PASS checks.

The GLM Astropy comparison moves from zero of four new tests and sixty-three of sixty-eight regression tests passing to all passing in the external verifier. However, the condensed successful trace shows dependency recovery and submission without explicitly showing the targeted local test command claimed by the prose. External success is established in the displayed summary; the stronger local-verification narrative is not fully visible there.

There are further artifact ambiguities. MiniMax's prose mentions separate diff and test subagents, while the displayed retained code shows one empty-diff subagent and a testing instruction. Initial SWE instructions refer to a FAIL-TO-PASS test specified in the problem; exact visibility of such test information must be pinned for benchmark parity. None of these discrepancies alone proves a runtime bug, but they matter to reproducing the mechanism.

## AppWorld edits: retrieve complete state and obey completion semantics

All three models learn some combination of pagination and completion-contract guidance. MiniMax adds a state auditor and time-boundary reminders. Qwen and GLM distinguish action-only tasks, which use null/no answer in this benchmark API, from information requests, which require the extracted value.[^8]

MiniMax's payment example initially requests money from seven participants; after reading all relevant transaction pages and applying the time boundary, it recognizes four already-paid participants and requests only the remaining three. Qwen's music example retrieves library pages and follows per-song details to answer a play-count query instead of returning null. GLM's transaction example expands from ten first-page records to all twelve and uses the required null completion.

These are simulated benchmark examples, not authorization to contact people or mutate real accounts. More importantly, the gain combines correct retrieval and correct protocol completion. GLM's plotted completion-only branch already reaches 76.4% from 44.4%; pagination alone reaches 74.7%, and the merged version reaches 85%. Much of the large gain repairs a sparse harness's interface semantics rather than proving broader reasoning growth.

The printed “retrieve all pages” rules assume particular pagination behavior. Real APIs may use cursors, limits, changing snapshots, or explicit totals. Everdict should encode complete-state evidence appropriate to the actual interface rather than hardcode AppWorld's page-index convention everywhere.

## Search trajectories, implementation gaps, and useful negative evidence

The nine evolution plots contain accepted branches, rejected trials, and explored endpoints; they are not nine independent replications of one optimizer. Some lower-scoring accepted nodes originate from older parents, showing why the parent of each candidate matters more than its position in evaluation order.[^7]

The plots support retaining branch evidence, but the full mapping from this branching search to the single-current-harness pseudocode is underspecified. The initial code listing also defines multiple hooks while its shown construction call passes only the base system prompt and backend. The surrounding adapter wiring for other hooks is not fully displayed. Reproduction needs the exact executed source, not only isolated helper definitions.

No dedicated ablation isolates clustering, proposal diversity, minimality, repeated evaluation, or the gate. Rejected edits are valuable evidence about attempted approaches; without their full diffs and split outcomes, they do not establish why each idea failed. A selected trace's narrative should remain separate from measured task results.

## Everdict proposal: use the audit structure with a stronger confirmation boundary

A directly useful candidate record would connect the verifier symptom, proposed mechanism, supporting trace spans, declared edit surface, parent digest, built artifact digest, split-wise outcomes, and actual adoption decision. Keep the symptom factual and the mechanism explicitly hypothetical. This aligns with Everdict's planned separation between driver-owned search and platform-owned evidence integrity.

An initial experiment can compare ordinary driver reflection, structured failure clusters, and clustered proposals with an archive of rejected attempts. Match total computation and keep the evaluator fixed. Measure confirmation-set completion, task-family regressions, unsupported diagnoses, unnecessary artifact edits, and improvement per search cost.

Selection can use the paper's two-split rule, but campaign settlement should use a separate frozen confirmation set. Individually accepted edits must be rebuilt together and evaluated as the exact merged artifact before promotion. Preserve task-level regressions even when aggregate counts improve, and retain neutral or rejected candidates for future search without making them active.

These are proposed Everdict changes, not already implemented behavior. The strongest lesson is that an edit needs inspectable evidence and an evaluated artifact; the paper's own false middleware diagnosis demonstrates why a successful endpoint cannot validate every explanation used to reach it.
