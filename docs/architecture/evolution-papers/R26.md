# R26 — SealQA: reasoning when retrieved evidence is misleading

SealQA studies factual questions for which ordinary search produces ambiguity, obsolete information, false premises, or plausible but irrelevant results. It is a benchmark, not a method for evolving an agent. Its relevance to Everdict is diagnostic: a larger evidence store and longer reasoning can reinforce the wrong conclusion unless the system resolves conflicts and checks which claim each source actually supports. This note uses arXiv 2506.01062v4 and distinguishes the original evaluation from models tested after the dataset became public.[^1]

## The underlying problem and related research

Retrieval-augmented generation usually assumes that retrieving relevant text helps answer a question. That assumption fails when relevance to the words differs from relevance to the intended entity, time, or scope. Several pages can repeat the same error, and an authoritative page can discuss the wrong version of an event. More retrieved text can therefore increase confidence without increasing correctness.

Related work isolates entity ambiguity, contradictions between credible sources, counterfactual context, unanswerable requests, and reliance on parametric versus retrieved knowledge. SealQA combines these issues in naturally occurring fact-seeking questions. It complements SimpleQA's factuality focus, BrowseComp's difficult browsing, FRAMES' multi-document reasoning, and academic benchmarks such as GPQA and HLE. Its questions are intentionally adversarially selected, so it measures a difficult failure region rather than typical search traffic.[^2]

## Dataset construction and the three variants

Six NLP researchers develop the benchmark over eight months. Drafting takes roughly 45 minutes per question, with total effort above an hour after review and revision. Each question must have a single unambiguous answer, supporting webpages, and an explanation. Two or more graduate-level reviewers inspect it before expert approval. The authors check links, answer validity, and clarity and record an effective year and future review date for changing facts.[^3]

There are five overlapping question categories: advanced reasoning, entity/event disambiguation, temporal tracking, cross-lingual reasoning, and false-premise detection. Their shares are 72.4%, 58.3%, 13.7%, 5.5%, and 4.3%; these do not sum to 100 because questions can require several capabilities. Topics include science/technology, sports, entertainment, politics, and history/geography. Questions average 31 tokens, so difficulty does not come from a long request alone.

The three variants have different experimental roles:

| Variant | Questions | Construction and intended test |
|---|---:|---|
| Seal-0 | 111 | Retain questions that selected models fail across 10–15 attempts, with and without search |
| Seal-Hard | 254 | Include Seal-0 plus difficult candidates that did not meet its all-fail threshold |
| LongSeal | 254 | Pair each Seal-Hard question with one helpful document and many difficult distractors |

Seal-0's construction models include GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, their mini variants, and Llama-4-Scout. Their zero scores on the selected set are partly built into the selection process. They should not be interpreted as unbiased estimates of those systems' ordinary factual accuracy.

For LongSeal, the authors retrieve top Google results, older results restricted to before 2023, and results for three related queries generated by GPT-4o mini. They deduplicate, remove documents above 10,000 tokens, and use a model to filter negatives that might reveal the answer. The gold document comes from human-provided evidence. The collection has over 7,600 documents, with up to fifty candidate hard negatives per question. The assertion that only the gold document supports the answer depends on this filtering; it is not a formal guarantee.

## Evaluation protocol, grading, and freshness

Models answer with temperature zero when configurable and their default otherwise. GPT-5 and the OpenAI o-series use a fixed temperature of one. Reasoning effort is high unless a comparison varies it. Some systems use built-in browsing; others receive Google results through FreshPrompt or self-ask. FreshPrompt injects retrieved evidence, while self-ask decomposes the question into subquestions before searching. These conditions change the retrieval process and available tools, not just the model.[^4]

A GPT-4o mini grader sees the question, reference, and response and labels it correct, incorrect, or not attempted. The answer must contain the requested information and remain consistent; a correct opening followed by a conflicting conclusion fails. Two authors independently review 100 answers, reconcile disagreements, and obtain 98% agreement between the reconciled human labels and the grader. This is a limited audit, not proof that every grade is valid or that humans independently agreed at 98%.

The dataset includes never-changing, slowly changing, and quickly changing facts: 31.1%, 43.7%, and 25.2%. Too-frequently changing answers are excluded. Nevertheless, a live search result and its correct answer can drift. Reproducing a run requires the question version, answer's effective date, model snapshot, and retrieved evidence. The authors explicitly acknowledge possible contamination and direct dataset access for post-release agentic models.

## Main results: search helps some systems and harms others

| System and condition | Seal-0 without search | Seal-0 with search | Seal-Hard without search | Seal-Hard with search |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GPT-4.1, built-in search | 0.0% | 0.0% | 15.0% | 20.5% |
| GPT-4.1-mini, built-in search | 0.0% | 0.0% | 13.8% | 11.8% |
| DeepSeek-R1-671B, FreshPrompt | 5.4% | 1.8% | 22.4% | 11.0% |
| Qwen3-235B-A22B, FreshPrompt | 0.0% | 5.4% | 4.3% | 11.4% |
| GPT-5-high, tested after release | 15.3% | 43.2% | 37.8% | 63.8% |

These are Table 1 values. The original o3-high with built-in search reaches 14.4% and 32.7% on Seal-0 and Seal-Hard. GPT-5's later improvement is substantial, but its exposure opportunity differs; it should not be presented as a clean private-test comparison.[^5]

DeepSeek-R1's search degradation is especially informative. FreshPrompt improves its rapidly changing and 2025-specific questions, yet hurts static and older questions: the reported never-changing subset falls by 17.7 points and the pre-2024 subset by 20.8 points. Retrieval can repair missing recent knowledge while damaging an already available correct answer. Self-ask is generally better than direct retrieval prompting, but the paper still finds vulnerable reasoning models.

The source has numerical inconsistencies worth preserving. Table 1 gives o3-mini-high Seal-Hard 12.6% without search and 14.2% with it, whereas Table 6 uses 14.6% and 12.2%; the prose broadly describes search harming o3-mini. Table 6 also uses different DeepSeek-R1 baseline values from Table 1. These rows cannot establish one uniform paired effect without the corresponding run configurations. The DeepSeek Table 1 degradation and effort experiments remain explicit evidence, but they do not resolve those mismatches.

Category breakdowns show particular difficulties with cross-lingual questions, false premises, and recent facts. These categories are small and overlap; a difference of one answer can materially change their percentages. Open-weight models also abstain more often in the reported answer-type analysis, so accuracy alone does not distinguish confident error from non-attempt.

## Reasoning effort and repeated sampling

Increasing the configured effort does not consistently improve Seal-0 results. At low, medium, and high effort, o3-mini obtains 1.8%, 2.7%, and 1.8%; o4-mini obtains 6.3%, 5.4%, and 4.5%; o3 obtains 11.7%, 17.1%, and 14.4%. The paper reports a similar lack of reliable monotonic scaling for later GPT-5 systems.[^5]

A proposed explanation is that longer reasoning amplifies misleading evidence. The sample outputs support the possibility: a model can identify a false premise and then reinterpret the question to provide a plausible name anyway, or notice the correct exception before concluding that none exists. These are illustrative trajectories, not a causal experiment proving that chain length itself causes failure.

At medium effort, five samples with an oracle that counts any correct answer yield 9.0% for o3-mini and 16.2% for o4-mini on Seal-0. This is pass-at-five style success, not the accuracy of an actual selection policy. A deployed agent still needs to identify which answer to trust and pay for all attempts. o3 is omitted from this experiment because of API cost.

## LongSeal: distractor count, position, and gold-only evidence

LongSeal varies the number of hard negatives across 12, 20, and 30 and moves the gold document through the context. Average prompt lengths are 27.6K, 54.5K, and 70.1K tokens. For 128K-window models, 100%, 99.2%, and 96.7% of examples respectively fit, so the largest condition is not uniformly free of capacity effects.[^6]

With gold evidence first, GPT-4.1-mini accuracy falls from 32.7% to 29.9% to 29.5%. GPT-4o-mini falls from 24.0% to 6.3% to 3.9%, and Llama-3.2-11B from 10.2% to 2.0% to 2.4%. Distractor count and total text length grow together, so this does not independently identify their effects. It does show that a large advertised context window does not guarantee useful evidence selection.

The positional curves lack a clear classic U-shaped lost-in-the-middle pattern. GPT-4.1-mini is relatively stable across positions, while Llama-4-Scout slightly favors later placement. This is evidence about the tested models and document construction, not proof that positional bias has disappeared universally.

Removing all distractors still leaves substantial errors: gold-only accuracy is 48.0% for GPT-4.1, 56.7% for o3-mini, 33.5% for Llama-4-Scout, and 49.2% for DeepSeek-R1 in Table 6. Retrieval failure therefore cannot explain everything; the model must interpret and reason from relevant evidence too. The differing baseline rows noted above limit precise decomposition into separate retrieval and reasoning losses.

## Human comparison and qualitative analysis

Five graduate-level NLP researchers who did not annotate the dataset answer fifty Seal-Hard questions, with Google access and a fifteen-minute limit per question. They then receive five curated URLs, one helpful and four misleading. Mean accuracy rises from 38.8% in open search to 50.4% with those URLs; the best human reaches 64.0% and 72.0%. On the same fifty questions, o3-high obtains 28.0%.[^7]

The oracle-URL condition follows open search, so familiarity and additional effort accompany improved evidence access. Its gain is not a randomized estimate of retrieval quality alone. Moreover, 52.8% of human answers arrive within five minutes, and those answers are correct 53.0% of the time: quick completion is not uniformly reliable.

The qualitative audit examines 100 responses across six model/condition combinations. It describes outdated knowledge, better citation behavior with built-in search, missed reasoning paths, rejection of valid answers, and repetitive reconsideration. These observations help form hypotheses but do not provide representative prevalence estimates for every failure mechanism.

## An Everdict experiment centered on evidence conflict

A useful candidate change is a conflict-resolution procedure that records entity, time, scope, source, and supported claim before synthesizing an answer. Compare raw retrieved evidence, a compact evidence summary, and a maintained claim map with explicit contradictions and supersession. Keep executor, search budget, and allowed information fixed. This is a proposal derived from SealQA, not a method tested in the paper or already implemented in Everdict.

Use a frozen document snapshot first, separating source drift from the candidate's reasoning. Then run a distinct live-search experiment with retrieval timestamps and archived evidence. Split by underlying fact/entity family, not merely paraphrased question. Report correctness, abstention, confidently wrong answers, source coverage, cost, and outcomes for false-premise and temporal subsets.

The curator may correct a derived claim map but must retain the raw evidence and the history of why a claim was superseded. Store scope and effective dates so that a newer statement about a different entity does not overwrite an older valid fact. Confirmation tasks should test cases where extra evidence is actively misleading as well as cases where new evidence is necessary. A system that improves only when documents agree has not demonstrated robust evolution under conflict.
