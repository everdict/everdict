# R09 — LiveMathematicianBench: testing the structure of research mathematics

LiveMathematicianBench turns recent research theorems into difficult five-choice questions. Its contribution is a refreshable construction and diagnostic evaluation pipeline, not a theorem-proving agent or skill optimizer. The initial hard split contains 177 questions from papers published between November 2025 and February 2026. This explanation follows arXiv 2604.01754v1 and its construction, validation, and evaluation appendices.[^1]

## Problem and neighboring research

A model can perform well on a familiar competition benchmark through a mixture of reasoning, memorized solutions, and recognizing answer patterns. Research mathematics adds specialized definitions and subtle conditions that may not resemble competition exercises. The authors ask whether a model understands the logical structure of a recent theorem, and whether a high-level proof strategy helps it distinguish closely related claims.

The related-work discussion traces increasing difficulty from GSM8K and MATH to graduate-level ARB/GHOSTS and competition collections such as OlympiadBench, PutnamBench, and Omni-Math. MiniF2F instead emphasizes formal verification. First Proof and FrontierMath use newly authored research-level problems, requiring substantial expert work. This benchmark takes a different operational route: reuse the stream of recent papers, then construct and audit questions automatically with human oversight. Fresh publication dates reduce some contamination risks but do not prove that all underlying concepts are new to a model.[^2]

## Principle: logical form rather than mathematical field alone

The taxonomy has thirteen forms: constructive algorithms, asymptotic limits, equivalence, classification/bijection, existence, existential–universal statements, implication, independence/consistency, bounds, nonexistence, uniqueness, universal statements, and universal–existential statements. A category describes what the claim asserts, not whether the topic is geometry or number theory.

For example, “for every input there is a suitable parameter” has the form ∀x∃y P(x,y). “There is one parameter suitable for every input” is ∃y∀x P(x,y), a stronger requirement in general. Swapping the quantifier order can preserve almost every word while changing the theorem. This illustrative example explains why surface similarity is a weak test of comprehension. The paper's category prompts target comparable distinctions in actual theorems.[^3]

## Construction: from a paper to a self-contained theorem

The seven-stage pipeline begins by retrieving `math.*` papers for a target month and recording their identifiers and source links. It downloads LaTeX sources, resolves included files, removes comments, and reconstructs the document. A rule-based extractor searches the introduction for a primary theorem, preferring theorem environments over auxiliary results. An LLM fallback handles unconventional formatting.

Custom macros are expanded and internal references resolved. Context recovery first ranks introductory paragraphs using term overlap, definition cues, mathematical content, and proximity; a second pass searches the entire paper for definitions and setup. Referenced material is added within a character budget. Another model summarizes the proof's strategic steps, and a classifier assigns the logical type. These operations make downstream questions tractable, but each can introduce semantic errors; an extracted theorem is not automatically a formally checked theorem.

## Construction: adversarial options and quality filters

A category-specific prompt generates a research-style question and its intended answer. It should isolate a decisive feature such as a bound, parameter dependence, quantifier, or uniqueness condition. A repair stage adds missing definitions without intentionally revealing the answer. The proof sketch then guides four distractors using controlled numerical/logical perturbation, weakening, strengthening, or confusion between related properties.[^3]

A weaker option can be mathematically true yet fail a question asking for the strongest justified conclusion. Therefore “incorrect choice” does not always mean “false proposition.” Likewise, a claim stronger than the cited theorem is not proven false merely because the theorem does not establish it. Establishing unique answerability requires mathematical validation of the question, not just labels produced by a generator.

Some items replace the explicit theorem answer with a meta-option asserting that another option holds but a stronger result is available. The intention is to obstruct direct option substitution and require comparison of claim strength. This is called substitution-resistant design. It adds a distinctive task demand; poorer performance can reflect that demand as well as weakness in the underlying mathematics.

Quality scoring assigns 0–2 points each for avoiding answer leakage, avoiding tautology, generative pressure, and distractor quality, for a total of 0–8; the methods text states a minimum threshold of 5. A stem-only model attempts the question without choices, and a second judge filters cases where it already recovers the answer. Finally, both original and regenerated choice sets are tested by a frontier calibration model. Per source theorem, the pipeline keeps a high-quality variant that the calibrator answered incorrectly; the source is removed if every variant was solved. This deliberately selects a hard distribution and can disadvantage the calibration model. It is not random sampling of all research mathematics.

## Human validation and evaluation protocol

A research mathematician designed the prompts and audited intermediate examples, including roughly ten per logical category in relevant upstream checks. The final 177 questions were reviewed for fidelity to the theorem, self-containedness, answer uniqueness, and distractor quality. LaTeX compilation and repair check notation rendering. Human review and rendering checks improve quality without supplying machine-checked proofs of every answer.[^4]

The baseline evaluation supplies the stem and five shuffled choices. A second mode additionally supplies the source-derived proof sketch. The default prompt excludes the stored source context and sketch. Released evaluation scripts support Azure, Anthropic, and vLLM backends with reproducible option shuffling. Grading extracts a final boxed letter, with fallback letter parsers, and compares it with the shuffled correct label. Thus a correct internal derivation with an incorrectly parsed final response can still count as failure.

The scripts retain responses, latency, and prompt/completion/reasoning-token information. Repeated samples are stored; the leading result is the first sample rather than an automatic majority vote. All models were evaluated without sketches, while sketch evaluations concentrate on the strongest systems because of budget. This asymmetry matters when reading the comparisons.[^5]

## Main results and diagnostic experiments

The strongest overall result is Gemini-3.1-pro-preview at 43.5%, followed by GPT-5.4 high at 41.8%; GPT-5.4 medium reaches 41.2%. Category analysis finds no universal winner. Gemini is relatively strong on equivalence and classification/bijection, while GPT-5.4 variants are stronger on implication, universal statements, and bounds. Categories with fewer than ten examples are grouped in one figure, so fine-grained estimates can be noisy.[^6]

| Analysis | GPT-5.4 high | Gemini-3.1-pro-preview high | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Overall, no sketch | 41.8% | 43.5% | Final hard benchmark |
| Original-choice subset | 52.2% | 67.4% | Explicit-answer format subset |
| Substitution-resistant subset | 30.6% | 17.6% | Different format subset; five-choice chance is 20% |
| Overall, with sketch | 53.7% | 57.1% | Additional source-derived strategic information |

The original-choice and substitution-resistant rows evaluate different subsets of the final collection, not a fully paired experiment that flips the format for every identical question. Their gap is consistent with format sensitivity, but item composition also contributes. GPT-5.4's relative stability changes the interpretation suggested by aggregate rank alone.

Adding proof sketches increases GPT-5.4 high by 11.9 percentage points and Gemini by 13.6. Appendix analysis separates the choice styles: GPT gains more on the original-choice subset, while Gemini gains especially strongly on substitution-resistant questions. This supports the usefulness of strategic guidance in this setting. It does not show that the models independently discovered those strategies.

## Monthly variation, cost, and limitations

Monthly slices vary, with several models doing better on January 2026 papers than February papers. Different questions and sample sizes mean this is not evidence that a fixed model became worse over time. The token analysis gives roughly 15.3k average completion tokens for Gemini, 7.0k for GPT-5.4 high, and 3.8k for GPT-5.4 medium. Similar accuracy can therefore come with substantially different generation volume. Token counts are not dollar costs across differently priced models.[^6]

The initial 177 questions are a small, intentionally filtered sample. Generator and judge errors, answer-format artifacts, overlapping logical categories, and calibration-model selection affect validity. A post-cutoff date is only useful relative to a known model snapshot; later API training or benchmark exposure can invalidate that protection. Finally, selecting a correct option is narrower than constructing a complete proof, discovering a new theorem, or having a formal verifier check it.

## Implications and a proposed Everdict experiment

This paper suggests evolving tests as well as procedures, while keeping the two evidence streams separate. A driver can derive contrastive cases from failed traces: change a quantifier, remove a required assumption, alter a boundary value, or swap a related property. Such changes probe a skill's applicability more directly than renaming variables. The generated case needs its own validation before it can become trusted evidence.

For Everdict, keep generator/calibrator tasks in training or selection. Freeze separate confirmation cases before using their outcomes for adoption. Compare a baseline skill with an evolved skill on matched ordinary and adversarial variants, recording exact prompts, answer parsers, disclosed hints, and per-category results. Run with and without strategically informative hints as explicitly different conditions. Measure false confident answers as well as accuracy.

WikiSkill uses this benchmark with train/validation/test partitions of 35/18/124 questions. Its appendix's competition-style description should not replace the benchmark's actual research-theorem construction. The relevant evolution lesson is conditional knowledge: a useful skill should preserve the assumptions under which a rule holds, and the evaluator should test those boundaries rather than only familiar successes.
