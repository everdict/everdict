# R23 — Trace2Skill: consolidating verified experience into portable procedures

Trace2Skill turns many success and failure traces into one reusable skill directory. It collects experience with a frozen model, diagnoses individual traces, and merges their lessons hierarchically instead of editing the skill after every incoming example. Its strongest insight for Everdict is that evidence quality and consolidation both matter: a plausible explanation from a log can be wrong, and individually useful patches can interfere when combined. This note follows arXiv 2603.25158v5, including its expanded transfer and comparison experiments.[^1]

## Problem and surrounding research

A human-written skill can help one executor and distract another. A skill drafted from a model's general knowledge can omit the practical details revealed by real artifacts: stale formula caches, wrong output ranges, destructive workbook rewrites, or missing verification. Trace2Skill asks whether those recurring details can be distilled into a portable procedure rather than retained as a large collection of episode-specific memories.

The method connects skill evolution such as EvoSkill, episodic reflection/retrieval such as ReasoningBank, and reusable executable/procedural memory. It differs from policy training because model weights stay fixed, and from runtime memory retrieval because the resulting SKILL.md is supplied directly. This is a particular compression strategy, not a claim that raw evidence or retrieval is always unnecessary.[^2]

The task is assumed to have a known relevant skill. Appendix I preloads SKILL.md, bypassing ordinary metadata-based discovery; auxiliary references and scripts still need to be found and used. The experiments therefore measure quality of a supplied skill, not the complete search/load/use pipeline studied in the wild-skills literature.

## Stage 1: collect a fixed experience corpus

A ReAct-style agent runs each evolution task with the initial skill and records the instruction, tool interactions, output artifact, and binary outcome. Success and failure traces form separate pools. Because every trajectory uses the same initial skill, the pool can be collected in parallel without later traces depending on earlier edits.[^3]

Two initializations are tested: deepening Anthropic's human-written xlsx skill and creating a skill from a weak parametric draft. In the main experiment, the draft is generated by Qwen3.5-122B. A 35B author still performs its own trajectory analysis and merging, but the shared seed's origin should not be erased by the headline “100% self-evolution.”

The distinction from online evolution is important. Batch consolidation freezes the evidence-generating policy for a round, which makes the corpus easier to compare. It may also miss failures that only appear after the skill changes. An additional collection round could address that, but the primary three-stage pipeline should not be described as an unbounded autonomous learning loop.

## Stage 2: asymmetric success and failure analysis

Success analysts use a single-pass workflow to extract broadly useful behavior. Failure analysts can inspect actual files, compare outputs with ground truth, locate a responsible decision, implement a minimal fix, and re-evaluate it. If a failure cannot be explained through this process, it is excluded from the failure-patch pool. Both roles then propose concise changes to the original skill.[^3]

The failure analyst's ground-truth access is explicit and appropriate to the evolution split. It is stronger feedback than an ordinary runtime agent receives and must not be confused with ground-truth-free reflection. A validated repair supports a local mechanism, but does not prove that every general rule inferred from that repair will transfer.

Excluding unexplained failures improves the evidential basis of retained patches, while introducing selection bias toward failures the analyst can repair. Difficult unresolved cases remain important evidence even if they do not become executable advice. Everdict should preserve them in the raw ledger rather than allowing “not understood” to mean “not recorded.”

## Stage 3: hierarchical consolidation and edit guards

Analysts produce patches independently against the same initial skill. A merge operator deduplicates equivalent edits, resolves contradictions, retains complementary insights, and promotes recurring patterns into general procedures. It merges batches of up to 32, repeating until one patch remains. References can hold infrequent details so the main document stays focused.[^3]

The final proposal becomes a diff with deterministic checks for valid targets, nonoverlapping line edits, and skill format. Appendix I additionally requires creation of a reference file and insertion of its link to be kept or dropped together. These guards protect structural consistency; they cannot prove the content is correct or that two nonoverlapping edits are behaviorally compatible.

The same author model supplies trajectories, analysis, and merging, with instruct mode for interactive ReAct and thinking mode for single-call operations. The setup uses 128 parallel analysts on eight-A100 nodes, with no separate tool-call cap. Reported skill-generation latency benefits from that parallelism. The entire research program, including exploration, consumes roughly 20,000 GPU-hours; a three-minute merge pipeline is not the cost of all experiments.

## Spreadsheet splits, metrics, and a membership caveat

SpreadsheetBench-Verified's 400 examples are split 200 for evolution and 200 for testing. Appendix G clarifies that the first slice contains 125 sheet-level and 75 cell-level tasks, while the held-out slice is cell-only. This is a meaningful distribution shift, not a random balanced split. Main results average seeds 41, 42, and 43.[^4]

Vrf is official instance accuracy on the held-out verified subset. Soft and Hard use the broader SpreadsheetBench protocol: partial test-case success versus requiring all associated cases to pass. The paper reports 2,529 broader evaluation examples after removing 200 evolving examples, plus 2,810 WikiTableQuestions and 1,585 HiTab examples converted into spreadsheets for out-of-domain transfer.

The 2,529 count equals the original benchmark's 2,729 cases minus 200. The text says evolving problems are removed, but this arithmetic alone does not establish that every sibling workbook from an evolution instruction is also excluded. Before using the broader scores as evidence of instruction-family-disjoint transfer, audit the exact parent IDs and case membership. This is an unresolved reporting/membership question, not a confirmed leakage finding.

The summary Avg gives equal weight to in-distribution and out-of-distribution groups across both user models. It is not a pooled success rate over all individual examples, and a large OOD improvement can coexist with a decline on a particular spreadsheet metric. Appendix A reports standard deviations, including some large individual-column variation even when the aggregate is stable.

## Main spreadsheet results and cross-model effects

The human-written skill raises 122B Vrf from 27.67% without a skill to 48.33%, but lowers 35B from 19.00% to 9.67%. This is a clear portability failure in the seed itself.[^5]

| Author and variant | 122B user: Vrf / Soft / Hard | 35B user: Vrf / Soft / Hard |
|---|---:|---:|
| Human-written seed | 48.33 / 36.30 / 17.03 | 9.67 / 13.03 / 3.37 |
| 122B deepening, errors | 65.83 / 46.60 / 27.43 | 36.67 / 22.47 / 6.23 |
| 122B deepening, combined | 69.83 / 47.17 / 29.53 | 30.83 / 21.87 / 5.17 |
| 35B deepening, combined | 55.00 / 40.17 / 21.20 | 29.67 / 18.80 / 5.73 |

These absolute values reconstruct the paper's signed-delta rows from their stated baseline. Error-only transfer can exceed combined transfer for a particular receiver: the 35B user benefits more from the 122B error-only skill than from its combined skill. Combining evidence is generally useful, not uniformly optimal.

The 35B-authored combined deepening skill has the highest overall Avg, 40.97 versus the seed's 26.93, largely because it improves the smaller receiver's OOD table reasoning. Creation also helps, especially the 122B-authored combined variant's Avg gain of 18.62 over its parametric seed. Yet its 122B Soft score declines 0.63 points. Report the cell-level tradeoffs rather than calling every evolution a win on every task.

Cross-family experiments use Gemma-4-31B-it and GPT-5.5-high, both as skill authors and users of Qwen-authored skills. They show beneficial transfer in the tested settings. An additional cross-model induction experiment lets those authors inspect Qwen traces; GPT-5.5-high's combined deepening reaches 68.00% Vrf for the Qwen122B receiver. This separates the model that generated evidence, the model that wrote the skill, and the model that executes it.

## Math and document-image experiments

Math skills are created from 400 DAPO questions, tested on 100 disjoint DAPO questions and thirty AIME 2026 problems. AIME uses avg@8, averaging attempts rather than counting any success among eight. Agents retain a command-line Python tool.[^6]

For 122B-authored error skills, 122B DAPO improves 91.0→95.0 and AIME 90.4→93.3; the 35B receiver improves 89.0→94.0 and 83.3→88.3. Combined skills improve the 35B receiver further on those main cells but reduce 122B AIME by 1.2 points. The 35B success-only skill also has negative or zero math deltas. Failure evidence is the more stable math signal here, not every type of experience in every domain.

DocVQA uses fifty evolution examples and 5,299 held-out document-image questions. It reports ANLS, a normalized string-similarity measure, and a thresholded accuracy at ANLS≥0.5; the latter is not exact match. The 122B-authored combined skill raises its own ANLS from 0.6300 to about 0.8834 and accuracy from 70.27% to 92.52%. The 35B-authored combined skill raises its own accuracy from 73.17% to 92.00%. All listed DocVQA variants improve their respective no-skill baselines, though the best transferring variant depends on author and receiver.

## Parallel versus sequential editing

The comparison reuses the trace pool and harness, varying whether edits are applied one trace at a time, four at a time, or after parallel analysis. In spreadsheet error-only deepening, the 122B Vrf scores are 61.83 for sequential-one, 59.00 for sequential-four, and 65.83 for parallel. Skill generation takes approximately sixty, fifteen, and three minutes.[^7]

Parallel is not best on every transfer metric: the 35B user's Soft/Hard are 23.83/10.57 for sequential-one versus 22.20/8.20 for parallel. The method buys substantial latency improvement with a favorable but nonuniform quality tradeoff. The comparison table's receiver results combine author settings as reported; do not assume every cell represents transfer of the same 122B-authored artifact.

Math ablations report parallel 95.0/91.7 for 122B DAPO/AIME and 94.0/88.3 for 35B, with two-minute generation versus 3.8/25.9 minutes for sequential batches four/one. The appendix's math baseline and some AIME cells differ from the main table, so these are their own reported comparison runs. DocVQA parallel reaches 92.52% accuracy versus 91.05/90.80 sequential-one/four, with 4.8 versus 35.9/5.2 minutes.

The complexity argument is about sequential dependency depth under enough workers: one parallel analyst stage followed by merge layers. An agentic analyst can make several tool/model calls, so “three stages” should not become “only three LLM calls in total.” Total work, critical-path latency, and infrastructure concurrency are separate costs.

## Consolidated skill versus retrieved memory

A ReasoningBank-style baseline stores lessons from the same traces and retrieves the top one using Qwen3-Embedding-8B. On same-model spreadsheet deepening, its Vrf is 56.00/20.50 for 122B/35B, versus consolidated combined skills at 69.83/29.67. Math and DocVQA comparisons also favor the chosen consolidated variants.[^7]

For DocVQA, ReasoningBank accuracy is 90.90/89.62 versus 92.52/92.00 for Trace2Skill. Math error-memory retrieval reaches 94.0/90.8 on the 122B DAPO/AIME comparison versus 95.0/91.7 for Trace2Skill. These support this consolidation protocol over this top-one retrieval baseline. They do not rule out larger retrieved sets, task-aware memory synthesis, or retaining raw traces behind a curated entry point.

The paper excludes WikiTQ/HiTab from the retrieval comparison because their queries are semantically distant from spreadsheet-editing queries. That is a scope choice, not a proof that retrieval is inapplicable to OOD tasks in general.

## Evidence-aware analysis and learned procedures

Agentic error analysis exceeds log-only analysis on Avg in three of four author/initialization blocks. The exception is 122B-authored creation, where log-only analysis has slightly higher Avg, 26.60 versus 26.02, through OOD results despite weaker spreadsheet scores. The interactive condition has both richer evidence and more computation; the experiment does not isolate those two benefits completely.[^8]

A qualitative audit of 33 shared failures finds strong agreement on four and clear disagreement on eighteen. When parse-error messages appear, log-only analysis attributes the primary cause to them in 57% of cases versus 14% for artifact-aware analysis. One example's actual artifact is already correct despite a log-based diagnosis of several failures. The lesson is to verify the final artifact and evaluator result before turning an error message into persistent advice.

Across 323 patch records, the most frequent learned themes are formula recalculation/read-back (55.1%), choosing a workbook-preserving write path (54.8%), explicit output verification (42.7%), and safe structural edits (16.4%). Shares overlap because one patch can support several themes. Thirteen auxiliary reference files retain uncommon details.

A concrete mechanism is deleting rows from bottom to top so earlier deletions do not shift later target indices. Another is recalculating formula values before reading cached outputs. These need applicability conditions: a generated suggestion to replace VBA with Python or formulas with fixed values may violate a task that explicitly requires reusable macros or live formulas. A benchmark-effective workaround is not automatically a universal user-facing rule.

## Greedy selection and Bayesian subset search

Greedy selection considers five high-support patches with at least five source traces. On a 32-task validation subset of the evolving set, it measures both adding a patch alone and removing it from the full proposed set. Patches with a negative effect in either test are discarded; the remaining scores are summed, with coverage breaking ties. Only the best patch is added per iteration.[^9]

The curves improve early but remain below full consolidation. Qualitative traces show simultaneous fixes and regressions, including one step with 57 fail-to-pass versus 21 pass-to-fail changes and another with 26 versus 46. Repeated overlapping themes also make new patches redundant. Local usefulness is not additive, and the small validation set may select noisy winners.

The Bayesian-optimization variant restricts the universe to at most fifteen high-support patches and searches binary inclusion vectors. It evaluates empty, singleton, five random mixed, and full subsets, then uses a TPE-style good-versus-bad model with a top-20% good group, Beta smoothing 1, and exploration mixture 0.1. Each round samples 500 proposals and evaluates eight, for at most four rounds or three without improvement. The size penalty is zero.

Compared with all error patches, subset selection raises Vrf 65.83→69.83, Soft 46.60→47.27, and Hard 27.43→28.90. WikiTQ improves slightly while HiTab declines 43.45→43.38. Every subset requires another materialized skill and validation run. This is useful evidence for interaction-aware search under a budget, not evidence that selection is free or always improves every distribution.

## Broader document tasks and whole-system comparisons

PDF evolution uses VRDU Registration Forms and transfers to VAREX Flat, improving 76.9→85.3. The reported row uses the strongest validated source split; the selection details matter when assessing selection bias. PPTX evolves on TSBench decks and tests on disjoint decks, improving 72.5→88.8. DOCX evolves on generated generic document operations and evaluates all 64 convertible OfficeBench DOCX subtasks with a 100-turn cap, improving 79.7→87.5.[^10]

Whole-system comparisons adapt XSkill, EvoSkill, and SkillGen to Qwen122B and the verified 200/200 split. Vrf is 23.0 for XSkill, 27.5 for SkillGen, 59.5 for EvoSkill with ReAct, 33.5 for EvoSkill through Claude Code, and 65.83/69.83 for Trace2Skill error/combined. Adaptations include different retrieval, iteration budgets, proxies, and acceptance thresholds. These are results of those transplanted implementations, not the native methods' maximum capability. The large EvoSkill harness gap itself demonstrates the confounding.

An external skill-creator baseline uses Opus 4.6 medium in Claude Code. Averaged over four author/user pairings, deepening changes 29.00→23.33 versus Trace2Skill error at 48.63; creation changes 23.17→22.50 versus 32.25. Some individual skill-creator cells improve. The average does not establish that general-purpose skill creation never works, and its resource/feedback protocol differs from the proposed distributed pipeline.

## Everdict proposal and evidence boundary

A suitable first Everdict experiment would compare sequential curation, batch consolidation of the same traces, and a budget-matched retrieved-memory baseline. Keep the evidence pool, executor, evaluation split, and output budget fixed. Separate success-derived and verified-failure-derived proposals, retain unresolved failures, and record source trace IDs for every merged rule. Measure final task success, family regressions, advice size, generation work, and wall-clock latency separately.

Candidate edits should preserve reference/link pairs and exact bytes, while independent checks establish behavioral validity. A curator may propose a broadly worded rule, but the driver should materialize and evaluate the resulting whole candidate. Selection of individual patches cannot replace confirmation of their combined effect. Rare high-impact failures should remain reachable even if frequency-based merging keeps them out of the main summary.

Ground truth used by the analyst belongs to the declared training disclosure. Selection and confirmation evidence must remain governed by the platform, and a runtime skill cannot rewrite the authoritative task contract. These are proposed applications to Everdict's documented first evolution loop, not implemented changes. The study is valuable evidence for portable procedural consolidation, with unresolved family membership, budget, and configuration differences explicitly retained. Results have not been independently reproduced here.
